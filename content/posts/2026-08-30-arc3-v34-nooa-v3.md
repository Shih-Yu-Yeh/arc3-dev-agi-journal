## TL;DR

Fixed V31's wiring (selective timeout 900 to 1200, max 2 extensions per level). 456 events mention NOOA. LB 2.24, +0.73 over V33 but still -0.32 below V24's 2.56.

## Context

V33's NOOA v2 regressed to 1.51. The diagnosis: the supervisor's strategy switching was too aggressive, causing oscillation. V34 added hysteresis (15min/20cooldown/3max-switches) and selective timeout (analyzer_timeout 900 default, but supervisor can request 1200 extension on stagnation, max 2 extensions per level).

The hypothesis: with controlled extensions, NOOA can recover above V33 without the oscillation cost.

## Technical Choice

NOOA v3 architecture (V31 done right):
- M1. NOOAMemory: two-tier (intra-game + cross-game), append-only (same as v2)
- M3. NOOASupervisor: hysteresis 15/20/3 + SELECTIVE TIMEOUT 900 to 1200, max 2 extensions per level

Two key differences from V33:
1. Selective timeout: instead of always extending, the supervisor extends only on stagnation detection.
2. Max 2 extensions per level: prevents infinite extension loops.

## Outcome Analysis

LB 2.24, +0.73 over V33's 1.51 but -0.32 below V24's 2.56.

The selective timeout helped: V34 recovered most of V33's regression. But NOOA is still net-negative compared to V24's no-modules config.

The 0.32 gap is the cost of NOOA's overhead. Even with controlled extensions, the modules add per-action latency that reduces the number of actions per game.

V34 is the best NOOA version. But "best NOOA version" is still worse than "no NOOA at all." The lesson: fixing module wiring does not help if the modules themselves are net-negative.
